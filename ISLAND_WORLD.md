# 100 Steps to Life — The Island World

## Core Decision

The ten islands are not decorative levels. Each is a **memory environment** for ten days of learning: one visual place, one emotional atmosphere, one landmark, and one recurring cue. The interface always keeps the route and the current lesson more prominent than scenery.

All ten islands belong to one ocean world. At the beginning, the user sees the current island clearly, the next island as a misted silhouette, and the distant Summit as a quiet promise. As an island is completed, its route becomes solid, its bridge or passage appears, and its landmark gains a small, calm completion signal. The world should feel mature, illustrative, and tactile—not cartoon-like or overloaded with props.

## Shared Interface Language

Every island uses the same progression grammar. It has a single broad silhouette, one landmark, ten stamped lesson nodes, one muted accent colour, and a short two- to four-word title. Lesson cards inherit only the island’s accent, landmark glyph, and environmental texture; they do not become separate visual systems.

The user should never need to decode a new map. A completed node is always a filled route stamp, the current node is always a gold-rimmed waypoint, and a locked node is always a low-contrast contour stamp. Island-specific art creates memory, while the shared route grammar keeps the product usable.

## Island Completion and World Merge

Finishing ten days triggers a brief transition: the active route line extends across water, a bridge/path/passage resolves, and the camera settles on the next island. This should stay under two seconds and can be skipped or reduced for motion-sensitive users.

At full completion, the ocean gaps do not disappear through a dramatic collision. Instead, the completed bridges, waterways, and contour lines finally reveal themselves as a single connected world. The Summit beacon changes from distant promise to an accessible central quest. The **Final Test** then appears as one deliberate route marker, assessing judgment and application across the full course rather than simple lesson recall.

## Visual Constraints

- One major landmark per island; no collections of competing props.
- The route and next actionable lesson always receive the strongest contrast.
- The Lantern Gardens use peaceful geometry, water, and light—not cartoon or decorative sacred architecture.
- Island art is background atmosphere and a memory cue, not a source of dense UI text.
- Morning, Night, and Green modes recolour the same world; they do not create three different maps.
