# Source research: self-discipline principle

## Working conclusion

The wording recalled by the user is attributed by Brian Tracy to **Elbert Hubbard** and is amplified in Tracy's *No Excuses!: The Power of Self-Discipline*.

The best-supported full version is:

> “Self-discipline is the ability to make yourself do what you should do when you should do it, whether you feel like it or not.”

## Verified sources

1. Brian Tracy's official article, *How to Develop Self-Discipline to Succeed*, presents the sentence as his “favorite definition” of self-discipline. The article frames the idea as self-mastery: learning the relevant skills and consistently applying them across life.
   - https://www.briantracy.com/blog/personal-success/self-discipline/

2. In the introduction of *No Excuses!*, Tracy recounts asking the industrialist Kop Kopmeyer for the most important success principle. He writes that Kopmeyer answered that the principle had been stated by **Elbert Hubbard**, then gives the same definition. Tracy immediately calls it “the key to personal greatness” and argues that discipline lets people turn their talent and intelligence into progress, while its absence leaves even otherwise advantaged people below their potential.
   - https://archive.org/stream/brian-tracy-no-excuses-the-power-of-self-discipline-2011/brian-tracy-no-excuses-the-power-of-self-discipline-2011_djvu.txt

3. Google Books lists Brian Tracy's *No Excuses!: The Power of Self-Discipline* (Vanguard Press, 2010), including a leading chapter titled “The Miracle of Self-Discipline” and sections that apply discipline to personal success, business, and “the good life.”
   - https://books.google.com/books/about/No_Excuses.html?hl=en&id=-KzXJgHu0KgC

## Attribution note

Brian Tracy explicitly attributes the sentence to Elbert Hubbard in *No Excuses!*; he is the author the user had in mind. The original printed primary source within Hubbard’s own work has not yet been independently located, so any scholarly product citation should acknowledge that the attribution is verified through Tracy’s account rather than through a recovered Hubbard publication.

## Product implication

The phrase is valuable as an underlying principle for *Hundred Steps to Life*, but it should be rewritten in the product's own voice rather than presented as a standalone attributed quotation until attribution is resolved.
